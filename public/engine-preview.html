SELECTED TRANSMISSION PART

How an automatic changes gears

  1. The engine drives the torque converter, which transfers torque toward the gear train.
  2. The controller determines the appropriate operating ratio from vehicle conditions.
  3. The hydraulic system applies and releases the required friction elements.
  4. The planetary gear sets provide the resulting ratio and deliver torque toward the driveline.

Lock-up operation can reduce converter slip once conditions allow.

SELECTED PART

How it works

  1. The engine’s drive belt turns the pulley and rotor.
  2. The rotor’s magnetic field moves past the stationary stator windings, generating AC electricity.
  3. Rectifier diodes convert that AC output into DC.
  4. The regulator manages rotor field current to help control output for the vehicle’s electrical system.

Reference: DENSO alternators

How it works

  1. Sensors send information about engine position, air, temperature and other operating conditions.
  2. Input circuits condition those signals so the controller can process them.
  3. The processor uses its software and calibration data to calculate commands.
  4. Output drivers control suitable actuators, while communication circuits exchange information with other modules.

SELECTED BRAKE PART

How braking works

  1. Pressing the pedal applies force to the master cylinder, assisted by the booster in this example.
  2. The master cylinder creates pressure in the brake fluid. Pipes and hoses carry that pressure to the wheel brakes.
  3. Caliper pistons press the pads against the rotating discs. Friction converts the vehicle’s motion into heat.
  4. Wheel-speed sensors let ABS detect an approaching wheel lock. The hydraulic unit can reduce and reapply pressure at the affected wheel.
